# Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) in Malaysia — Market Research Report

*Compiled June 2026 from ~60 sources across three research tracks: players, market/economics, and regulation. Where official statistics and market-research-firm estimates conflict, the official Ringgit (RM) figures are treated as load-bearing.*

---

## 1. Executive Snapshot

- **A big, fast-growing, highly concentrated market.** BNPL transaction value hit **RM12 billion in 2024** (149M transactions, ~2× 2023) and **RM21.3 billion in 2025** (243M transactions, +78%). Yet it remains tiny as a credit risk — outstanding balance was **RM4.9 billion at end-2025, just ~0.3% of household debt**.
- **Three players own >90% of users:** **SPayLater 56.5%**, **Atome 26.5%**, **Grab PayLater 8.3%** — together ~95–97% of transaction value.
- **The game just changed:** the **Consumer Credit Act 2025** came into force **1 March 2026**; **BNPL licensing opens 1 June 2026**. BNPL went from *unregulated* to *licensed* almost overnight.
- **The model is fragile:** ShopBack PayLater, Hoolah, and Pace **exited in 2024**; IOUpay / myIOU **collapsed in 2023** (alleged fraud). Thin margins + high delinquency shake out weak players.

## 4. Business Model & Economics

- **Primary revenue = Merchant Discount Rate (MDR):** the merchant pays a cut per transaction. Industry benchmark **~2–8%** (Malaysian providers don't publicly disclose exact rates; Shopee raised its seller-side fee to 4.5% in 2025).
- **Secondary:** late fees (RM10–23), interest on longer installments (1.25–2.5%/mo), interchange.
- **Most short-tenure BNPL is 0% interest to the consumer**, so MDR + late fees carry the model.
- **Merchant value prop:** the Zalora × Grab PayLater case showed **+20% new shoppers, +15% checkout completion, +32% basket size**. That is why merchants tolerate the MDR.
- **The catch:** thin margins + loss rates of 25–35% on bad cohorts. ShopBack's exit and IOUpay's collapse show how fragile the economics are.

## 5. Regulation — the Defining 2025–2026 Shift

This is the single most important development for anyone entering the space.

**Consumer Credit Act 2025 (Act 873):**

- Passed Dewan Rakyat **21 July 2025** → gazetted **31 Dec 2025** → in force **1 March 2026**.
- Creates the **Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna (SKP) / Consumer Credit Commission** as the regulator (evolved from the interim CCOB Task Force, which ran public consultations in 2022–2023).
- **BNPL = a "credit business" requiring a LICENCE** (the heavier tier, not just registration).

**Timeline for new entrants:**

- **Licensing opens 1 June 2026** (Part V), with a **transition window to 31 December 2026** to apply.
- Phased rollout: SKP first regulates 6 previously-ungoverned activities (BNPL, factoring, leasing, debt collection, impaired-loan acquisition, debt counselling), then absorbs other regulators' remits by ~2031.

**What a new BNPL licensee must meet:**

- **Minimum shareholders' fund: RM2 million** (well-attested from consultation papers — *confirm against final gazetted subsidiary regulations*).
- **Fit-and-proper** controllers / directors / management.
- **Mandatory affordability checks** before granting / raising credit (salary slips, EPF, bank statements + credit-bureau data).
- **Full upfront disclosure** of all fees / late charges.
- **Late fees cannot compound; no interest charged on top of late fees** (exact RM / % cap not yet public — likely in subsidiary regs).
- **Mandatory reporting** of BNPL borrowing to credit agencies (e.g. CTOS) via a centralised database.
- **Fair debt-collection conduct** rules; the original provider stays liable for its collectors.

## 6. Key Takeaways for a New Entrant

1. **The window is now regulated.** Anyone launching BNPL in Malaysia from mid-2026 needs an **SKP licence + RM2M capital + affordability / credit-reporting infrastructure**. The "just launch an app" era is over.
2. **Incumbency is brutal.** Top 3 hold >90% of users; the long tail is littered with exits. Differentiation (Shariah, offline / DuitNow QR reach, embedded in a specific vertical) matters more than another generic Pay-in-3.
3. **The economics are merchant-funded and thin.** You need volume and low default — which now requires the very affordability checks the law mandates.
4. **Open questions to verify before acting:** exact late-fee cap, final capital threshold, and SKP's licence-application checklist (all pending in subsidiary regulations — confirm directly with SKP).

## 7. What It Would Take for *Your* App to Offer a PayLater Feature

This section maps the findings onto a practical build decision. There are three routes, from lightest to heaviest.

### Route A — Partner with a licensed BNPL provider (fastest, lowest risk) ✅ *recommended starting point*
Integrate an existing licensed player (Atome, SPayLater, Boost PayFlex, Grab PayLater, Fave) at your checkout. **They hold the licence and carry the credit risk; you just enable the payment option.**

- **Licence needed by you:** None — the partner is the licensed credit provider.
- **Cost:** You pay the **MDR (~2–8%)** per transaction; no capital requirement.
- **Time to launch:** Weeks (an integration, not a licence).
- **Trade-off:** You don't own the customer credit relationship or the lending margin — you're buying conversion uplift.
- **Best when:** You want the *conversion / basket-size* benefit without becoming a lender.

### Route B — Become a licensed BNPL provider yourself (own the credit) ⚠️ *heavy*
Offer your *own* installment credit to users. Under the Consumer Credit Act 2025 this makes you a **credit business requiring an SKP licence.**

- **Licence needed:** SKP **credit-business licence** (applications open 1 June 2026).
- **Capital:** **~RM2 million** minimum shareholders' fund (confirm final figure).
- **Obligations:** affordability checks, fit-and-proper management, upfront fee disclosure, late-fee caps (no compounding), credit-bureau reporting, fair debt collection.
- **Time to launch:** Many months (licence + risk infrastructure + capital).
- **Best when:** Lending margin is core to your business model and you have the capital and risk appetite.

### Route C — Closed-loop "pay later" for your *own* services only (grey-zone, verify) 🟡
If you only let customers defer payment for **your own services** (e.g. pay for a towing / roadside job over installments), this *may* sit closer to a deferred-payment / trade-credit arrangement than a licensed BNPL business — but **the Consumer Credit Act 2025 is broad, and "BNPL" is explicitly named as a licensable credit business.** Do not assume closed-loop exempts you the way it does for e-money wallets.

- **Action required:** Get a written legal/SKP determination before relying on this. The safe assumption is that **extending repayment terms to consumers = consumer credit = licensable.**

### Decision guide

| If you want to… | Use Route |
|---|---|
| Boost checkout conversion quickly, no lending | **A — partner** |
| Earn the lending margin, own the customer credit | **B — get licensed** |
| Let customers pay for *your* services over time | **C — verify with SKP first** |

### Recommended path for most apps
**Start with Route A** (partner integration) to capture the conversion benefit with near-zero regulatory burden, while you assess whether the lending margin in Route B justifies the RM2M capital + licensing load. Revisit Route B only once volume proves the unit economics.

> This is general guidance, not legal advice. The Consumer Credit Act 2025 and its subsidiary regulations are new (in force March 2026); confirm capital thresholds, fee caps, and your specific product classification directly with the **Suruhanjaya Kredit Pengguna (SKP)** or a Malaysian fintech lawyer before building.
