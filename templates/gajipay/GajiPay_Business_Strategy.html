Business Strategy & Corporate Proposal

GajiPay Sdn Bhd — an integrated Earned Wage Access, HR, and corporate compliance platform for the Malaysia SME market.

§ 01

Executive Summary

GajiPay Sdn Bhd is positioned to become Malaysia's leading integrated employee financial wellness and HR compliance platform, built around a flagship Earned Wage Access (EWA) "Advance Pay" product and cross-sold through four supporting service lines: HR Portal, Payroll Services, Company Secretary Services, and an Audit & Accounting referral channel.

Malaysia's 1.2+ million MSMEs — 97% of all business establishments, 48% of national employment, 39.5% of GDP — are structurally underserved: enterprise-grade HR suites like Employment Hero price them out or over-serve them, pure-play EWA fintechs (Paywatch, Payd, HariGaji) sell a single point-solution with no compliance stack behind it, and traditional company secretary, payroll, and audit firms remain paper-based, disconnected, and reactive.

GajiPay's strategy is to win the compliance relationship first, then unlock the fintech relationship. Every company in Malaysia must have a company secretary and run statutory payroll (EPF/SOCSO/EIS/PCB) — mandatory, sticky, recurring, and the wedge. Once GajiPay operates the payroll data pipe, it can safely and accurately calculate real-time earned-wage balances, the hardest technical and compliance problem in EWA — a data advantage competitors without a payroll book cannot easily replicate.

Load-bearing legal structure

GajiPay will not hold a moneylending license and does not intend to obtain one. Advance Pay is structured within the Moneylenders Act 1951 exemption for employer-facilitated financial assistance to employees, combined with strict adherence to Sections 22 and 27 of the Employment Act 1955 (advances capped at wages already earned; no interest charged) — the same structure used successfully by HariGaji/altHR and Paywatch, and reinforced by the AmBank–Ramssol "Pay Day Now" precedent.

This document sets out the market analysis, competitive landscape, differentiated positioning, prioritized service-line strategy, go-to-market plan, regulatory strategy, operating model, risks, and phased roadmap. Financial projections (P&L, cash flow, break-even, IRR) are intentionally scoped out and will follow as a separate financial model once this strategy is approved.

§ 03

Market Analysis

3.1 Malaysia macro context

  • MSMEs account for ~97% of all business establishments, ~48% of total employment, and contributed RM652.4 billion (39.5% of GDP) in 2024 — growing faster than the overall economy (5.8% vs. 5.1% national GDP growth).
  • This is GajiPay's addressable base: a huge, fragmented population of employers legally obligated to run payroll and retain a company secretary, but largely unable to afford — or uninterested in — enterprise HR software.
  • Malaysia's workforce skews heavily toward hourly, shift, and lower-to-middle income employees (retail, F&B, manufacturing, logistics, security, construction, healthcare support) — the primary beneficiaries of EWA and the most turnover-sensitive to cash-flow stress.

3.2 Earned Wage Access market

  • The global EWA market is projected to grow from roughly USD 8.8B (2026) to USD 52B by 2034; Southeast Asia is one of the fastest-growing regions, evidenced by Kredivo Group's acquisition of Indonesia's GajiGesa (350,000+ employees on platform) in February 2025.
  • Malaysia already has multiple live EWA players — Paywatch, Payd, HariGaji, and the newly launched bank-backed Pay Day Now — which validates demand and regulatory feasibility, but the market is still early and fragmented: no single player has become the default, and none bundles EWA with a full statutory compliance stack.
  • Employer-sponsored EWA (integrated with payroll, at no or low cost to the employer) is the dominant and fastest-growing global model, reinforcing GajiPay's payroll-led approach over a standalone consumer app.

3.3 Regulatory environment — summary, full treatment in §8

  • Employment Act 1955, s.22(1): an employer may not advance wages beyond what has already been earned — the legal ceiling on Advance Pay balances.
  • Employment Act 1955, s.27: employers are prohibited from charging interest on salary advances — GajiPay's revenue must be a flat processing/service fee.
  • Moneylenders Act 1951: employer-to-own-employee financial assistance, non-profit or reasonable-fee, is excluded from moneylending licensing — the load-bearing structure for Advance Pay.
  • PDPA 2010: GajiPay will process sensitive payroll, salary, and banking data at scale, requiring a robust data protection and consent framework from day one.

4.3 Company secretary / compliance competitors

  • Every Malaysian company must appoint an SSM-authorized company secretary within 30 days of incorporation (Companies Act 2016, s.235). Market retainer rates run RM60–150/month, with RM200–500 for annual return filings.
  • Digital company secretary platforms are digitizing incorporation, e-signature board resolutions, and compliance calendars, increasingly competing on flat, transparent pricing versus traditional firms.
  • None currently bundle payroll, HR, or EWA — the second half of GajiPay's white space.

4.4 Positioning summary

GajiPay is not trying to out-build Employment Hero's enterprise suite, nor out-fund Paywatch's regional EWA scale. GajiPay's wedge is being the only platform that bundles the mandatory compliance stack (Company Secretary + Payroll) with the financial wellness stack (EWA) for the Malaysian SME segment, with Audit & Accounting referral as a natural, low-effort extension once trust is established.

§ 05

SWOT

Strengths

  • Green-field brand, no legacy tech debt — payroll-EWA data pipeline designed correctly from day one
  • Bundled model creates stickier, higher-LTV customers than any single-service competitor
  • No moneylending license required — lower regulatory capital burden than a lending-licensed fintech

Weaknesses

  • New entrant, no brand trust, track record, or existing book of business
  • No banking/EMI license — reliant on a partner institution for fund flow and disbursement
  • Thin working-capital base versus bank-backed (AmBank) or well-funded (Paywatch: RM141M raised) rivals

Opportunities

  • 97% of Malaysian businesses are MSMEs; digital CoSec/payroll remains fragmented, no dominant one-stop player
  • Cross-sell flywheel: CoSec/Payroll → HR Portal → EWA → Audit referral, each layer raising switching cost
  • Partnership potential mirroring the Digi–altHR–HariGaji and AmBank–Ramssol precedents

Threats

  • Incumbent EWA players have first-mover distribution advantages through telco/bank partners
  • A bank-backed entrant (Pay Day Now) can undercut on trust and capital cost
  • Regulatory tightening could raise compliance costs — engage proactively, not reactively

§ 07

Service Line Strategy

7.1 Advance Pay (EWA) — the flagship

  • Mechanic: employees access a portion of already-earned wages after the 15th of each month, capped at a conservative percentage of wages earned to date — recommend starting at 30–40%, below Paywatch/HariGaji's 50% ceiling and above Pay Day Now's 25%.
  • Revenue: flat processing fee per withdrawal (benchmark RM2–5), paid by the employee or subsidized by the employer as a benefit — offer both models.
  • Dependency: requires accurate, real-time attendance/earned-wage data — built on GajiPay's own Payroll/HR data, not sold standalone in Phase 1.
  • Legal structure: GajiPay is the technology/servicing provider enabling the employer to advance already-earned wages, within the Moneylenders Act 1951 exemption. Disbursement runs through a partner bank/EMI, not GajiPay's balance sheet.

7.2 HR Portal — the acquisition and data wedge

Free-to-low-cost entry point (echoing altHR's bundling logic): leave/attendance management, employee self-service, org chart, basic performance tools. Not built to compete feature-for-feature with Employment Hero/Swingvy on day one — it exists to win the employer relationship cheaply, generate the data Advance Pay needs, and build the login habit that makes Advance Pay a one-tap experience.

7.3 Payroll Services — the compliance and data engine

Full statutory payroll processing (EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB/MTD, EA forms), priced per-employee/month, competitive with Talenox/HR2eazy/SQL Payroll benchmarks. Once GajiPay runs payroll, switching cost rises sharply across every other service line, and GajiPay holds clean first-party earned-wage data that pure-play EWA competitors must obtain via often-imperfect third-party integrations.

7.4 Company Secretary Services — the mandatory wedge

Every Sdn Bhd needs one — a compliance necessity, not a discretionary purchase, making it an efficient, low-CAC lead-generation and retention product. Bundle CoSec + incorporation + annual return filing at competitive flat-fee pricing (RM60–150/month retainer, RM500–1,500 incorporation, RM200–500 annual return) with a modern, digital-first client experience. CoSec is often the first vendor relationship a new Sdn Bhd forms — winning it early positions GajiPay to cross-sell Payroll, HR Portal, and eventually Advance Pay.

7.5 Audit & Accounting — referral revenue

GajiPay does not hold audit/accounting licenses and will not build this in-house; formal referral partnerships with licensed audit firms and chartered accountants earn referral/commission fees. This closes the "trusted back-office partner" loop at zero regulatory risk and near-zero delivery cost, and keeps GajiPay engaged with clients at audit season.

§ 08

Regulatory & Compliance Strategy

  • Commission a formal legal opinion before public launch confirming Advance Pay's structuring under the Moneylenders Act 1951 exemption and Employment Act 1955 ss.22/27 — specifically GajiPay's role as technology/servicing provider versus the employer as the party extending the advance.
  • Do not hold, lend, or float funds from GajiPay's own balance sheet. Structure disbursement through a partner bank or licensed e-money issuer under BNM's e-money framework — mirroring the AmBank–Ramssol precedent — both to avoid inadvertently triggering licensing thresholds and to build institutional trust.
  • PDPA 2010 compliance by design: formal data protection policy, encryption of salary/bank data at rest and in transit, documented consent flows, and a data retention/deletion policy.
  • Proactively engage BNM and relevant industry bodies (e.g., Fintech Association of Malaysia) as the EWA category matures, rather than waiting for reactive regulation.
  • AML/CFT screening on payroll accounts and disbursement flows, particularly at scale.

§ 09

Go-to-Market Strategy

9.1 Channels

  • Accounting & audit firm partnerships: high-trust referral channel into their SME client base for CoSec and Payroll.
  • SME associations & chambers of commerce (SME Corp Malaysia programs, FMM, sector-specific associations) for direct access to the target segment.
  • Bank and telco partnerships: the Digi–altHR–HariGaji and AmBank–Ramssol deals show distribution partnerships with institutions that already touch SMEs are the fastest way to scale trust and reach — pursue at least one within 12–18 months of launch.
  • Direct/inside sales targeting the primary segment sectors, leading with CoSec/Payroll (lower resistance) and Advance Pay as the expansion sell.
  • Digital marketing & SEO: Malaysian searches around "gaji," payroll calculators, and company secretary requirements show meaningful organic volume — a content and calculator-tool strategy can capture top-of-funnel demand cheaply.

9.2 Sequencing

  1. Land with Company Secretary + Payroll bundle (lowest resistance, mandatory need).
  2. Expand with HR Portal at low/no incremental cost to deepen the relationship and capture attendance data.
  3. Activate Advance Pay once payroll/attendance data is clean and the employer relationship is established.
  4. Extend with Audit & Accounting referral at each client's financial year-end.

§ 10

Marketing & Brand Strategy

Positioning: GajiPay is the back office that also looks after your people — not just another payroll vendor, and not just another salary-advance app.

Employer-facing message: "One partner for compliance and people — company secretary, payroll, HR, and financial wellness, without the enterprise price tag or the fragmented vendor list."

Employee-facing message: "Your money, when you need it — access wages you've already earned, transparently, with no interest and no debt."

Content strategy: SME compliance guides (incorporation checklists, EPF/SOCSO/EIS explainers, annual return deadlines), employer case studies quantifying turnover reduction from Advance Pay (industry benchmarks cited by competitors range 16–25%), and a free payroll/EPF/SOCSO calculator tool as an SEO and lead-gen asset.

Trust-building assets: transparent fee disclosure (flat fee, no interest — mirrored prominently against payday-loan alternatives), a published legal/compliance explainer on the Moneylenders Act exemption, and visible partner branding once a bank/EMI partnership is secured.

Website and full brand identity (logo, visual system) are explicitly out of scope for this document and will follow as Task 2 once this strategy is approved.

§ 11

Operations & Technology

  • Core platform: unified employer dashboard (payroll, HR, CoSec case management) and employee mobile/web app (payslips, leave, Advance Pay).
  • Payroll engine: native support for EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB/MTD, and EA form generation, with an audit trail suitable for statutory inspection.
  • Advance Pay ledger: real-time earned-wage calculation engine, hard-capped against s.22 Employment Act limits, reconciled daily against payroll run data.
  • Integration layer: APIs for the bank/EMI disbursement partner, plus import/export tooling so early clients on competitor payroll systems migrate without a hard cutover.
  • Security & compliance: PDPA-aligned data governance from day one; target ISO 27001 alignment once transaction volume justifies the cost.

§ 15

Conclusion & Next Steps

GajiPay's opportunity is not to out-compete any single incumbent at their own game — it is to be the first platform to seriously bundle Malaysia's mandatory SME compliance stack (Company Secretary, Payroll) with the fastest-growing employee financial wellness product in the region (Advance Pay/EWA), with Audit & Accounting referral as a natural extension. The legal path to Advance Pay without a moneylending license is well-precedented by HariGaji and Paywatch, and further legitimized by the AmBank-backed Pay Day Now launch — GajiPay's job is to execute that structure carefully, lead with compliance trust, and let the cross-sell flywheel convert a mandatory back-office relationship into a daily-engagement fintech relationship.
