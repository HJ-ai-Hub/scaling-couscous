## 1. Executive Summary

GajiPay Sdn Bhd is positioned to become Malaysia's leading **integrated employee financial wellness and HR compliance platform**, built around a flagship **Earned Wage Access (EWA) "Advance Pay" product** and cross-sold through four supporting service lines: HR Portal, Payroll Services, Company Secretary Services, and an Audit & Accounting referral channel.

Malaysia's 1.2+ million MSMEs (97% of all business establishments, 48% of national employment, 39.5% of GDP) are structurally underserved: enterprise-grade HR suites like Employment Hero price them out or over-serve them, pure-play EWA fintechs (Paywatch, Payd, HariGaji) sell a single point-solution with no compliance stack behind it, and traditional company secretary/payroll/audit firms remain paper-based, disconnected, and reactive.

GajiPay's strategy is to **win the compliance relationship first, then unlock the fintech relationship**. Every company in Malaysia is legally required to have a company secretary and to run statutory payroll (EPF/SOCSO/EIS/PCB). These are low-margin but mandatory, sticky, and recurring — they are the wedge. Once GajiPay operates the payroll data pipe, it can safely and accurately calculate real-time earned-wage balances, which is the single hardest technical and compliance problem in EWA. That data advantage becomes the moat competitors without a payroll book of business cannot easily replicate.

Critically, GajiPay will **not hold a moneylending license** and does not intend to obtain one. The strategy relies on structuring Advance Pay within the exemption under the **Moneylenders Act 1951** for employer-facilitated financial assistance to employees, combined with strict adherence to **Sections 22 and 27 of the Employment Act 1955** (advances capped at wages already earned; no interest charged). This mirrors the legal structure already used successfully by HariGaji/altHR and Paywatch in Malaysia, and is reinforced by the AmBank–Ramssol "Pay Day Now" precedent, which pairs a licensed financial institution with a payroll technology partner. GajiPay should adopt an analogous partnership posture — technology and payroll orchestration layer, with a licensed bank or e-money issuer partner handling any float, disbursement, or fund-holding function — and a formal legal opinion should be commissioned before public launch (see Section 8).

This document sets out the market analysis, competitive landscape, differentiated positioning, prioritized service-line strategy, go-to-market plan, regulatory strategy, operating model, risks, and phased roadmap. Financial projections (P&L, cash flow, break-even, IRR) are intentionally scoped out of this document and will be delivered as a separate financial model once this strategy is approved.

## 3. Market Analysis

### 3.1 Malaysia macro context

- MSMEs account for **~97% of all business establishments** in Malaysia, **~48% of total employment**, and contributed **RM652.4 billion (39.5% of GDP)** in 2024, growing faster than the overall economy (5.8% vs. 5.1% national GDP growth).
- This is the addressable base for GajiPay: a huge, fragmented population of employers who are legally obligated to run payroll and retain a company secretary, but are largely unable to afford — or uninterested in — enterprise HR software.
- Malaysia's workforce skews heavily toward **hourly, shift, and lower-to-middle income employees** (retail, F&B, manufacturing, logistics, security, construction, healthcare support) who are the primary beneficiaries of EWA and the most turnover-sensitive to cash-flow stress.

### 3.2 Earned Wage Access market

- The global EWA market is projected to grow from roughly **USD 8.8B (2026) to USD 52B by 2034**, and Southeast Asia is one of the fastest-growing regions, evidenced by Kredivo Group's acquisition of Indonesia's GajiGesa (350,000+ employees on platform) in February 2025.
- Malaysia already has multiple live EWA players (Paywatch, Payd, HariGaji, and the newly launched bank-backed Pay Day Now), which validates demand and regulatory feasibility, but the market is **still early and fragmented** — no single player has become the default, and none currently bundles EWA with the full statutory compliance stack GajiPay is proposing.
- Employer-sponsored EWA (integrated with payroll, at no or low cost to the employer) is the dominant and fastest-growing model globally, reinforcing GajiPay's payroll-led approach over a standalone consumer app.

### 3.3 Regulatory environment (summary — see Section 8 for full treatment)

- **Employment Act 1955, s.22(1):** an employer may not advance wages beyond what has already been earned. This is the legal ceiling on any Advance Pay balance and dictates GajiPay's post-15th-of-month / earned-days-worked calculation logic.
- **Employment Act 1955, s.27:** employers are prohibited from charging interest on salary advances. GajiPay's revenue on Advance Pay must therefore be structured as a **flat processing/service fee**, not interest — consistent with every credible competitor in market (Paywatch: flat RM2/transaction; HariGaji: RM15 first transaction/month, free thereafter; Pay Day Now: RM2–5/withdrawal).
- **Moneylenders Act 1951:** arrangements where an employer provides financial assistance exclusively to its own employees, on a non-profit or reasonable-fee basis, are **excluded from moneylending licensing**. GajiPay's role must be carefully structured as the technology/servicing layer enabling the *employer* to advance already-earned wages, not as a third-party lender to employees — this is the load-bearing legal structure for the entire Advance Pay product and should be locked down with counsel before launch.
- **PDPA 2010:** GajiPay will process sensitive payroll, salary, and banking data at scale, requiring a robust data protection and consent framework from day one.

## 4. Competitive Landscape

### 4.1 Direct EWA competitors

| Player | Model | Fee | Backing / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paywatch** | Leading SEA EWA by volume; app-based, employer API integration; withdraw up to 50% of earned wages | Flat RM2/transaction | RM141M Series A raised; expanding regionally (MY, PH, ID, KR) |
| **Payd** | Shariah-compliant EWA; on-demand access | Not fully disclosed | US$400K seed extension (2025); 100,000+ employees on platform; expanding to Thailand |
| **HariGaji (altHR/Digi)** | Bundled free into Digi's altHR HR super-app; up to 50% of earned wages | RM15 first withdrawal/month, free thereafter | First mover in Malaysia (2020); backed by Digi telco distribution |
| **Pay Day Now (AmBank × Ramssol)** | Bank-backed, integrated into Ramssol payroll; up to 25% of earned wages weekly | RM2–5/withdrawal | Newly launched (2026); strong trust signal from AmBank; targets SMEs to large corporates and public sector |

**Read-through:** every credible player has converged on the same legal/fee structure GajiPay must use (flat fee, no interest, capped % of earned wages), and the market leaders are increasingly the ones with a **distribution advantage from an adjacent core business** — Digi's HR platform, Ramssol's payroll base, AmBank's banking relationships. GajiPay's adjacent core business is its own payroll + company secretary book, which is a comparable (and for the SME segment, arguably stronger) distribution advantage.

### 4.2 HR / Payroll software competitors

| Player | Positioning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment Hero** | Enterprise-grade HR/payroll/benefits suite, AU-origin, MY presence | Priced from ~A$10/employee/month, 10-user minimum — often too expensive/complex for micro-SMEs; does not offer EWA or company secretary services |
| **Swingvy** | Design-forward HRMS for SMEs | Strong UX, no compliance bundle |
| **Talenox** | Cloud HRIS, MY payroll compliance | Payroll-only focus |
| **HR2eazy, BrioHR, SQL Payroll, Kakitangan** | Various SME-focused HR/payroll tools | Fragmented, feature-competitive, none bundle EWA + CoSec + audit referral |

**Read-through:** the HR software market is commoditizing on payroll compliance alone. None of these players combine mandatory compliance (company secretary, audit) with financial wellness (EWA) — this white space is GajiPay's core differentiation.

### 4.3 Company secretary / compliance competitors

- Every Malaysian company must appoint an SSM-authorized company secretary within 30 days of incorporation (Companies Act 2016, s.235). Market retainer rates run **RM60–150/month**, with **RM200–500** for annual return filings.
- Digital company secretary platforms (e.g., Osome, Biztory, Foundingbird-style players) are digitizing incorporation, e-signature board resolutions, and compliance calendars, and increasingly compete on flat, transparent pricing versus traditional law/accounting firm secretarial arms.
- **None of these digital CoSec platforms currently bundle payroll, HR, or EWA** — this is the second half of GajiPay's white space.

### 4.4 Competitive positioning summary

GajiPay is not trying to out-build Employment Hero's enterprise suite, nor out-fund Paywatch's regional EWA scale. GajiPay's wedge is being the **only platform that bundles the mandatory compliance stack (CoSec + Payroll) with the financial wellness stack (EWA) for the Malaysian SME segment**, with Audit & Accounting referral as a natural, low-effort extension once trust is established.

## 5. SWOT

**Strengths**
- Green-field brand with no legacy technology debt; can design the payroll-EWA data pipeline correctly from day one.
- Bundled model creates a stickier, higher-LTV customer than any single-service competitor.
- No moneylending license required — lower regulatory capital burden than a lending-licensed fintech.

**Weaknesses**
- New entrant with no brand trust, track record, or existing book of business — must earn compliance trust before employers extend a fintech relationship.
- No banking/EMI license — reliant on a partner institution for fund flow and disbursement, creating a dependency.
- Thin working-capital base versus bank-backed (AmBank) or well-funded (Paywatch: RM141M raised) competitors.

**Opportunities**
- 97% of Malaysian businesses are MSMEs, and the digital CoSec/payroll category remains fragmented with no dominant "one-stop" player.
- Cross-sell flywheel: CoSec/Payroll → HR Portal → EWA → Audit referral, each layer increasing switching cost.
- Partnership potential with banks, telcos, or SME associations mirroring the Digi–altHR–HariGaji and AmBank–Ramssol precedents.

**Threats**
- Incumbent EWA players (Paywatch, HariGaji) have first-mover distribution advantages through telco/bank partners.
- A bank-backed entrant (Pay Day Now) can undercut on trust and capital cost.
- Regulatory tightening (e.g., BNM formalizing EWA-specific rules) could raise compliance costs across the industry — GajiPay should engage proactively rather than reactively (see Section 8.4).

## 6. Target Segments

**Primary (Phase 1):** Malaysian SMEs with 20–500 employees in sectors with high proportions of hourly/shift, lower-to-middle income staff and elevated turnover sensitivity:
- Food & beverage / retail chains
- Manufacturing (factory floor staff)
- Logistics, warehousing, last-mile delivery
- Security services
- Construction and facilities management
- Healthcare support / eldercare staffing agencies

**Secondary (Phase 2):** Micro-enterprises (<20 employees) via a lightweight, self-serve HR Portal + CoSec bundle, and larger corporates (500+ employees) via direct sales once GajiPay has reference customers and a proven compliance track record.

**Why this segmentation:** these sectors combine (a) statutory payroll complexity that creates CoSec/payroll pull, and (b) a workforce demographic with demonstrated appetite for EWA (per Paywatch/HariGaji adoption patterns), maximizing the odds that a customer who signs up for compliance services converts into an EWA customer.

## 7. Service Line Strategy

### 7.1 Advance Pay (EWA) — the flagship

- **Mechanic:** employees access a portion of already-earned wages after the 15th of each month (aligned with mid-month cash-flow stress points), capped at a conservative percentage of wages earned to date (recommend starting at 30–40%, below Paywatch/HariGaji's 50% ceiling and above Pay Day Now's 25%, positioning GajiPay as balanced — generous enough to matter, conservative enough to be defensible to first-time employer clients and regulators).
- **Revenue:** flat processing fee per withdrawal (benchmark RM2–5, matching market), paid by the employee or subsidized by the employer as a benefit — offer both models so employers can choose their positioning (cost center vs. employee benefit).
- **Dependency:** requires accurate, real-time attendance/earned-wage data — this is why Advance Pay must be built on top of GajiPay's own Payroll/HR data, not sold as a standalone app to companies using a competitor's payroll system (at least not in Phase 1).
- **Legal structure:** GajiPay acts as the technology/servicing provider that enables the *employer* to advance already-earned wages to its own staff, falling within the Moneylenders Act 1951 exemption. Fund disbursement should run through a partner bank or licensed e-money issuer (see Section 8.3), not GajiPay's own balance sheet, at least until Phase 1 is proven.

### 7.2 HR Portal — the acquisition and data wedge

- Positioned as the free-to-low-cost entry point (echoing altHR's "free with EWA" bundling logic): leave/attendance management, employee self-service, org chart, basic performance tools.
- Purpose is *not* to compete feature-for-feature with Employment Hero/Swingvy on day one — it exists to (a) win the employer relationship cheaply, (b) generate the attendance/hours data Advance Pay needs, and (c) create the login habit that makes Advance Pay a one-tap experience for employees.

### 7.3 Payroll Services — the compliance and data engine

- Full statutory payroll processing (EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB/MTD, EA forms), priced per-employee/month, competitive with Talenox/HR2eazy/SQL Payroll benchmarks.
- This is the most defensible, highest-trust service line — once GajiPay runs payroll, switching cost rises sharply across all other service lines, and GajiPay has the clean, first-party earned-wage data that pure-play EWA competitors have to obtain via (often imperfect) third-party payroll integrations.

### 7.4 Company Secretary Services — the mandatory wedge

- Every Sdn Bhd needs one; this is a compliance necessity, not a discretionary purchase, making it an efficient, low-CAC lead-generation and retention product.
- Bundle CoSec + incorporation + annual return filing at competitive flat-fee pricing (benchmark: RM60–150/month retainer, RM500–1,500 incorporation, RM200–500 annual return) with a modern e-signature / digital-first client experience versus traditional firms.
- Strategic role: CoSec is often the *first* vendor relationship a new Sdn Bhd forms — winning it early positions GajiPay to cross-sell Payroll, HR Portal, and eventually Advance Pay as the company hires its first employees.

### 7.5 Audit & Accounting — referral revenue

- GajiPay does not hold audit/accounting licenses and will not build this in-house; instead, structure formal referral partnerships with licensed audit firms and chartered accountants, earning referral/commission fees.
- This closes the loop on the "trusted back-office partner" positioning at zero regulatory risk and near-zero delivery cost, and gives GajiPay a reason to stay engaged with clients year-round (audit season) rather than only at payroll cutoff or CoSec renewal.

### 7.6 Cross-sell flywheel

```
New Sdn Bhd incorporated
        │
        ▼
Company Secretary Services  ──(mandatory, low CAC)
        │
        ▼
Payroll Services  ──(as company hires staff)
        │
        ▼
HR Portal  ──(bundled/low-cost, drives engagement + data)
        │
        ▼
Advance Pay (EWA)  ──(flagship, highest engagement + revenue per user)
        │
        ▼
Audit & Accounting referral  ──(annual, margin-accretive, zero license risk)
```

Each stage raises switching cost and lifetime value; Advance Pay is the emotional, high-frequency touchpoint that keeps employees (not just the HR/finance decision-maker) engaged with the GajiPay brand daily.

## 8. Regulatory & Compliance Strategy

1. **Commission a formal legal opinion before public launch** confirming Advance Pay's structuring under the Moneylenders Act 1951 exemption and Employment Act 1955 ss.22/27, specifically addressing GajiPay's role as technology/servicing provider versus the employer as the party extending the advance.
2. **Do not hold, lend, or float funds from GajiPay's own balance sheet.** Structure disbursement through a partner bank or licensed e-money issuer (EMI) under BNM's e-money framework — mirroring the AmBank–Ramssol precedent — both to avoid inadvertently triggering licensing thresholds and to build institutional trust with enterprise clients.
3. **PDPA 2010 compliance by design:** formal data protection policy, encryption of salary/bank data at rest and in transit, documented consent flows, and a data retention/deletion policy, given GajiPay will hold some of the most sensitive personal data an employee has (salary, attendance, banking details).
4. **Proactively engage BNM and relevant industry bodies** (e.g., via the Fintech Association of Malaysia) as the EWA category matures, rather than waiting for reactive regulation — positions GajiPay as a compliance-first operator and provides early warning of any licensing changes.
5. **AML/CFT screening** on payroll accounts and disbursement flows, particularly once GajiPay handles higher transaction volumes.

## 9. Go-to-Market Strategy

### 9.1 Channels

- **Accounting & audit firm partnerships:** the reverse of the referral flywheel in Section 7.5 — small and mid-tier audit/accounting firms are a high-trust referral channel into their SME client base for CoSec and Payroll services.
- **SME associations & chambers of commerce** (e.g., SME Corp Malaysia programs, FMM, sector-specific associations for F&B/retail/logistics) for direct access to the target segment.
- **Bank and telco partnerships:** the Digi–altHR–HariGaji and AmBank–Ramssol deals demonstrate that distribution partnerships with institutions that already touch SMEs (business banking, SME telco packages) are the fastest way to scale trust and reach in this category — GajiPay should pursue at least one such partnership within 12–18 months of launch.
- **Direct/inside sales** targeting the primary segment sectors in Section 6, led with the CoSec/Payroll compliance offer (lower resistance) and Advance Pay as the expansion sell.
- **Digital marketing & SEO:** Malaysian searches around "gaji," payroll calculators, and company secretary requirements show meaningful organic volume (evidenced by tools like GajiMalaysia ranking for salary/EPF/SOCSO queries) — a content and calculator-tool strategy can capture top-of-funnel demand cheaply.

### 9.2 Sequencing

1. **Land** with Company Secretary + Payroll bundle (lowest resistance, mandatory need).
2. **Expand** with HR Portal at low/no incremental cost to deepen the relationship and start capturing attendance data.
3. **Activate** Advance Pay once payroll/attendance data is clean and the employer relationship is established — this is the monetization and brand-differentiation stage.
4. **Extend** with Audit & Accounting referral at each client's financial year-end.

- **Positioning:** GajiPay is the back office that also looks after your people — not just another payroll vendor, and not just another salary-advance app.

## 11. Operations & Technology

- **Core platform:** unified employer dashboard (payroll, HR, CoSec case management) and employee mobile/web app (payslips, leave, Advance Pay).
- **Payroll engine:** must natively support EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB/MTD, and EA form generation, with an audit trail suitable for statutory inspection.
- **Advance Pay ledger:** real-time earned-wage calculation engine, hard-capped against s.22 Employment Act limits, reconciled daily against payroll run data.
- **Integration layer:** APIs for bank/EMI disbursement partner, and import/export tooling so early clients on competitor payroll systems can still be migrated into GajiPay's HR Portal without a hard cutover.
- **Security & compliance:** PDPA-aligned data governance from day one; target ISO 27001 alignment (formal certification can follow once transaction volume justifies the cost) given the sensitivity of salary and banking data.

## 12. Risk Analysis

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Regulatory reclassification of EWA as lending | Formal legal opinion pre-launch; conservative fee structure (flat fee, no interest); proactive regulator engagement; partner-bank disbursement model |
| Employer fails to fund/reconcile advances at payroll run | Contractual reconciliation terms; disbursement via partner bank with employer-funded settlement account, not GajiPay balance sheet |
| Low differentiation if bundling doesn't convert to cross-sell | Sequence GTM to prove the CoSec→Payroll→HR→EWA flywheel with a pilot cohort before scaling spend |
| Incumbent distribution advantage (Digi/AmBank-backed rivals) | Pursue GajiPay's own bank/association partnership within 12–18 months; compete on SME-segment service quality and bundle economics rather than head-on brand spend |
| Data breach of payroll/salary data | PDPA-by-design architecture, encryption, access controls, incident response plan |
| Working capital / cash-flow strain from disbursement float | Structure disbursement through partner bank/EMI, not GajiPay capital, from day one |

## 14. Success Metrics (KPIs)

- **Compliance base:** # of active CoSec clients; # of active Payroll clients; net retention rate.
- **Cross-sell conversion:** % of CoSec/Payroll clients that activate HR Portal within 90 days; % that activate Advance Pay within 6 months.
- **Advance Pay engagement:** % of eligible employees who activate; average withdrawals per active user per month; take-rate on flat fees.
- **Employer outcomes:** measured turnover reduction among Advance Pay clients versus non-Advance-Pay clients (target: cite comparable 15–25% reduction benchmarks from competitor data once GajiPay has its own cohort data).

## 15. Conclusion & Next Steps

GajiPay's opportunity is not to out-compete any single incumbent at their own game — it is to be the first platform to seriously bundle Malaysia's mandatory SME compliance stack (Company Secretary, Payroll) with the fastest-growing employee financial wellness product in the region (Advance Pay/EWA), with Audit & Accounting referral as a natural extension. The legal path to Advance Pay without a moneylending license is well-precedented by HariGaji and Paywatch and further legitimized by the AmBank-backed Pay Day Now launch — GajiPay's job is to execute that structure carefully, lead with compliance trust, and let the flywheel in Section 7.6 do the work of converting a mandatory back-office relationship into a daily-engagement fintech relationship.
